History did not scream when it resisted.

It whispered.

Arun felt it in the way the morning resumed too smoothly, as if the pause had never occurred. The guards settled into their new positions with professional efficiency. Radios returned to their low murmur. The folder—his folder—was already being leafed through by an officer who would find nothing inside but blank pages.

A lie with weight.

Indira Gandhi walked again.

Arun exhaled slowly. His pulse still hammered behind his eyes, the lingering ache a reminder that he was not supposed to be here—not like this, not now. The system, the anomaly, time itself—whatever had carried him across decades—had not promised safety. Only opportunity.

And opportunity, he was learning, came with teeth.

Ripples

Something was wrong.

Not catastrophically. Not visibly. But wrong in the way a seasoned traveler sensed a delayed train before the announcement was made.

Arun noticed it first in Beant Singh’s posture.

He had shifted.

Not dramatically—just half a step back, a subtle recalibration that placed him slightly out of alignment with the other guards. It was the kind of adjustment no one questioned and everyone trusted.

But Arun had memorized this scene in another life.

Beant Singh had never stood there before.

A bead of sweat traced Arun’s spine.

This was the danger of interference: not changing the event, but changing how it arrived.

---

Memory vs Reality

Arun closed his eyes briefly.

In the original timeline, the first gunshot had come almost immediately—close, brutal, undeniable. What followed had been chaos measured in seconds.

Now, seconds stretched.

Indira paused to greet a gardener.
A smile flickered—polite, distant.
Her steps slowed.

Arun’s breath caught.

Delay was a double-edged gift.

He searched faces desperately—not for the assassins, but for doubt. For hesitation. For the smallest human fracture that might halt what history insisted must happen.

He saw none.

---

The Man History Forgot

“Hey.”

A hand gripped Arun’s arm.

Ravi Malhotra.

The junior officer’s expression was taut, eyes sharp with something between suspicion and fear.

“You,” Ravi said quietly. “This morning. You said—”

“I was wrong,” Arun replied immediately. Too quickly. “Just nerves. Big day.”

Ravi studied him, then glanced toward the Prime Minister. “You don’t look like a journalist.”

“I’m not.”

“Then what are you?”

Arun met his gaze. This man—this ordinary, unremarkable man—was now entangled in the anomaly with him.

“I’m someone who doesn’t want today to end the way it’s supposed to,” Arun said.

Ravi swallowed.

The radios crackled louder.

---

Fracture Point

It happened differently.

Not with the clean inevitability Arun remembered.

There was a shout—confusion before comprehension. Beant Singh moved first, not last. The spacing Arun had disrupted forced Satwant Singh to hesitate, his line of sight obstructed by a guard who had not been there before.

The first shot rang out—

—but it missed.

The sound tore through the morning like a rupture in the sky. Birds scattered. The gardener dropped his tools. Someone screamed.

Indira Gandhi turned.

For the first time, fear crossed her face.

History screamed now.

Chaos Is Not Victory

Arun lunged forward instinctively, Ravi shouting behind him. The guards surged, training colliding with shock. Orders overlapped. Someone fired again.

The second shot struck.

Then the third.

Arun saw Indira Gandhi fall—not where she was meant to, not how—but fall all the same.

The ground rushed up to meet her.

History corrected itself.

---

Aftermath in Motion

Time fractured into fragments.

Blood on white stone.
Hands pressing, shouting.
Sirens too early, too loud.

Arun stood frozen, the weight of failure crushing his lungs. He had bent the timeline, twisted it, delayed it—

—but not broken it.

Ravi stared at the scene, face ashen. “You said… you said it didn’t have to end like this.”

Arun’s voice barely carried. “I thought… maybe.”

Ravi looked at him then—not as a fellow bystander, but as something else. Something dangerous.

“You knew,” Ravi whispered.

Before Arun could answer, the world lurched violently.

The pain returned, sharper now—like being pulled backward through a narrowing tunnel. Sound collapsed. Color drained.

Arun felt the day tearing itself away from him.

---

The Cost of Trying

As consciousness faded, Arun saw one last thing.

Not Indira Gandhi.

But Ravi Malhotra, standing alone amid the chaos, holding the blank folder—his name now written across the cover in someone else’s hand.

History had accepted Arun’s intervention.

Just not in the way he had hoped.

It had traded one certainty for another.

And it had chosen a new witness.
