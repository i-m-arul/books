Ravi Malhotra would later struggle to explain the silence.

Not the absence of sound—there was plenty of that—but the kind of silence that arrived after something irreversible. The world continued moving around him, yet everything meaningful had already happened. Time, it seemed, had made its choice and walked away.

Indira Gandhi was gone.

The official words would come later, sharpened by protocol and repetition. For now, there was only the body covered hastily, the red spreading where it should not, the smell of gunpowder and disbelief.

And in Ravi’s hands—

The folder.

It was heavier than it should have been.

---

A Name That Did Not Belong

Ravi had picked it up without thinking. Training, reflex, a need to anchor himself to something solid. But when he opened it—expecting forms, schedules, bureaucratic nonsense—he found only blank pages.

Except for the cover.

Written in neat, unfamiliar handwriting was his name:

Ravi Malhotra

No designation.
No rank.
Just the name.

Ravi closed the folder slowly.

He felt watched—not by the guards, not by the investigators already swarming the scene, but by the moment itself. As if the day had noticed him noticing it.

---

The Man Who Vanished

They searched for Arun.

Of course they did.

Witnesses remembered him vaguely—a civilian, out of place, holding papers. Some recalled his face clearly; others swore they had never seen him at all.

There was no record.
No badge.
No past.

It was as if he had stepped out of a crack in the morning and stepped back in when finished.

Ravi did not tell anyone what Arun had said.

He did not say:

“I don’t want today to end the way it’s supposed to.”

Because the day had ended exactly as it was supposed to—
and yet, Ravi could not shake the certainty that it had not ended the same.

---

History’s New Shape

The reports would later note anomalies.

A missed first shot.
A delay of thirteen seconds.
A security repositioning that did not align with protocol.

Thirteen seconds was nothing.

Thirteen seconds was everything.

Rumors spread quietly through intelligence circles. Some spoke of hesitation, others of fate. A few—very few—wondered if the assassination had nearly failed.

Ravi wondered something else entirely.

What if it had failed?

And this was the correction.

Inheritance

That night, Ravi dreamed of blank pages.

They fluttered endlessly, flipping themselves, waiting for ink that never came. He woke drenched in sweat, the folder on his bedside table—he did not remember bringing it home.

When he opened it again, the pages were no longer empty.

One line had appeared, written in the same careful hand:

History resists force.
It yields only to witnesses.

Ravi’s breath caught.

This was impossible.

And yet, after that morning, so was everything else.

---

The Echo

In the days that followed, India burned.

Riots.
Retribution.
Silence enforced by fear.

Ravi watched the news with a growing sense of unreality. The violence felt… louder than it should have been. Faster. As if the country itself were compensating for something that had almost not happened.

An echo.

History replaying itself to ensure it was heard.

Ravi began to notice other fractures.

Newspaper headlines changed subtly between editions.
Conversations repeated with different endings.
Once, briefly, he heard a radio broadcast refer to Indira Gandhi surviving an assassination attempt—before correcting itself mid-sentence.

No one else seemed to notice.

The folder gained another line.

You delayed it.
Now you must carry it.

---

The Choice That Remains

Ravi understood then.

Arun had not failed.

He had passed the burden.

The anomaly—whatever it was—did not belong to saviors or heroes. It attached itself to those close enough to see the seam, but powerless enough to suffer it.

Witnesses.

Ravi stood at his window that night, the city below flickering with unrest, and realized with quiet terror that he could still feel the day pulling at him—like a loose thread.

The timeline was no longer fixed.

It was echoing.

And somewhere, in some other version of this morning, Arun might still be walking toward the gate, holding a folder, hoping for a different ending.

Ravi closed the folder.

Tomorrow, he would begin writing.
