Ravi Malhotra did not go to work the next morning.

The city was still smoldering—smoke rising in thin, exhausted columns, sirens dissolving into distant echoes—but Ravi remained in his apartment, seated at the small dining table, the folder placed carefully before him like an artifact recovered from a disaster site.

He had spent the night awake.

Watching.
Listening.
Testing.

Time behaved strangely when he did.

---

The Experiment

At 08:30 a.m., Ravi turned on the radio.

The newsreader announced a curfew extension in East Delhi.

Ravi switched the radio off.

He waited exactly thirty seconds and turned it on again.

The announcement changed.

Now the curfew was under review.

Ravi’s pulse quickened.

He repeated the test with the newspaper delivery. The first edition listed casualties as estimates; the second, delivered minutes later, named them precisely—too precisely.

As if the facts were solidifying in real time.

Ravi opened the folder.

A new line appeared, the ink dark and unmistakable:

Observation alters consolidation.
Do not look too closely.

Ravi laughed—a short, brittle sound.

“Too late,” he said to the empty room.

A Name Reappears

He found Arun again by accident.

Or perhaps nothing was accidental anymore.

In a government archive, buried in security logs from the previous day, Ravi found an entry that had not been there before:

Unidentified civilian — access query logged at 09:22 a.m.

No photograph.
No address.

But next to the timestamp, a single word had been added in red ink:

Witness.

Ravi felt a chill settle in his bones.

He was not alone.

---

The Rule That Matters

That night, Ravi dreamed not of pages, but of mirrors.

Each mirror showed the same morning—Indira Gandhi walking, the guards shifting—but each reflection ended differently. In one, she survived. In another, Ravi was the one who fell. In a third, Arun stood still and did nothing at all.

Ravi woke with a certainty that frightened him.

The timeline was not singular.

It was selective.

And it responded not to action—but to intent.

The folder confirmed it when he opened it:

You cannot stop the event.
You can only choose who carries it forward.

Ravi sat back, the meaning sinking in.

Arun had chosen him.

The Second Intervention

Ravi left his apartment just before dawn.

The streets were quieter now, watched, measured. He walked with purpose—not toward a place, but toward a pattern. He sought moments that felt thin, fragile, unfinished.

He found one at a relief camp.

A Sikh family stood arguing with a police officer. Voices rose. Fear edged toward violence.

Ravi stepped in.

Not as an officer. As a witness.

He listened.

He repeated words back to the speakers—not changing them, just holding them long enough for meaning to surface. The argument slowed. The tension deflated.

No miracles. No headlines.

But the echo softened.

The folder warmed beneath his arm.

A new line formed:

This is how it is done.

---

Becoming

Ravi understood now what Arun had been.

Not a time traveler.
Not a savior.

A stabilizer.

Someone who absorbed history’s excess force so it did not tear itself apart.

Witnessing was not passive.

It was labor.

It demanded presence without control, courage without reward.

Ravi returned home exhausted, older than he had been the day before.

He opened the folder one last time that night.

The pages were no longer blank.

They were waiting.

---

The Long View

Outside, Delhi breathed unevenly.

Inside, Ravi picked up a pen.

History had not ended.

It had recruited him.

And somewhere—perhaps in another echo, perhaps in memory—Arun was finally resting, the burden passed cleanly at last.

Ravi began to write.
