This book is a work of fiction.
It is grounded in real events, real places, and real loss, but it does not claim to explain, accuse, or resolve them. Timelines have been compressed, characters combined, and perspectives imagined — not to alter history, but to examine how it is experienced.
The intention of this novel is not to revisit blame or advance theory. It is to explore a quieter question:
How do moments that change everything pass through ordinary hands without being stopped?
What you have read is not an answer.
It is an observation.
The facts of May 21, 1991, belong to public record, inquiry, and memory. This story belongs to the spaces between those records — the pauses, absences, and human limits that rarely survive official language.
If the book unsettles, it has done its job.
History rarely announces itself.
It arrives where it is permitted.
